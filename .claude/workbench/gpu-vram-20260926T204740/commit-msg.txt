Measure each pool item's VRAM and bound the width

GNU Time measures an item's RAM and cannot see the GPU, and GNU
Parallel has no --memfree for it. gpu_monitor.py samples nvidia-smi
over the item's process tree while it lives and writes <n>.gpu with
peak and mean VRAM, peak GPU use and the sample count, in three
states that are never collapsed: a measurement (zero included),
absent (no nvidia-smi, no file) and error (tried and failed).

step_report publishes max and median VRAM and counts errors apart.
The pool history keeps each run's VRAM peak, and the pool launches at
min(configured, RAM, VRAM) width, each bound being (free - reserve) /
(peak x margin) with the reserve subtracted from what is free. Since
free VRAM moves while items start, each item waits for room for its
peak before starting: the admission half of Parallel's --memfree,
read from /usr/bin/parallel 20231122. Its other half, killing the
youngest job, is not ported: it would kill a claude -p mid-request.

There is no GPU in this container; the suites use a fake nvidia-smi
and prove the mechanism, not nvidia-smi's accuracy.

Annulment: counting every GPU process instead of the tree, summing
free VRAM across GPUs, dropping the error record, skipping admission
or not applying the width each fail exactly their cases.
