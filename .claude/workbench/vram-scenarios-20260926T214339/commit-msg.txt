Test VRAM admission at three levels, not one

The fake nvidia-smi is a model of how we believe an NVIDIA GPU behaves;
a green suite against it proves the admission logic, not that the
model matches hardware. This separates the levels and gives each its
own evidence.

Level 1, the fake, now has state: free VRAM is the total minus what
live processes use, and a stepped allocator raises its use when the
test says so. test_gpu_scenarios checks, phase by phase, used,
pending, free and headroom while a reservation turns into use, plus
overshoot, SIGKILL before release, a zombie owner, foreign VRAM, and
two admissions released at once behind a barrier. Annulling the lock
admits both in every round; counting the whole reservation or losing
the descendants breaks the table; treating a zombie as alive keeps
its reservation. headroom is split out of _try_admit as the query.

The pool suite runs two real headless-pool processes on that GPU with
one shared ledger. Its first run failed: the history carried a VRAM
peak of 0 from a different item, so each item asked for nothing and
both ran together. The case now measures its own item first; with a
ledger per pool the two overlap again.

Level 2 is gpu_trace: record a stepped allocator's trace and compare
it with the fake's assumptions, naming the fake parameter a trace
contradicts (context_overhead_mib, hide_pids). The comparator is
tested by recording against a fake with overhead and hidden PIDs. The
real-GPU harnesses for levels 2 and 3 refuse with exit 2 and no count
here, where there is no GPU; that refusal is what this container can
test.
