Port the executable's lock for shared state

Pool workers write only their own files, but setups.jsonl, a
template's history and a step's commit and push are shared, and
nothing kept two writers apart: step_setup.register reads, checks and
appends with no lock.

shared_lock.py ports proper-lockfile as packaged in 2.1.282
(chunk-bzev8hcq.js, extracted to the bench with bin/binary): an atomic
mkdir of <file>.lock, an mtime heartbeat, a stale threshold, one steal
attempt, exponential retries and a compromised warning on release.
Two declared divergences: the lock carries owner.json, and a stale
lock whose owner is alive on this host is not stolen but refused with
its stdin_probe.

bin/binary reflow gains --root, like symbol, so a module of the
versioned corpus can be reflowed; it only read the live executable.

Annulment: without the live-owner guard, the heartbeat or an exclusive
mkdir, exactly their cases fail; without mkdir exclusivity six workers
that must count 150 leave 68.
